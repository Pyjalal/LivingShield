# LivingShield: judge narrative review across every discovered project

Prepared 21 October 2026, Malaysia time. This is a **narrative and relevance assessment**, not official judging, an award prediction, or a new full-function test of every product. The user confirmed the previously discovered projects as the comparison set. This review covers every repository in that set and every published project site; it does not claim a complete organiser roster.

LivingShield baseline: commit **1ede99c**, before the resulting narrative revision. References to its opening describe that reviewed baseline.

## The judge’s objection is valid

The reviewed opening asks a judge to admire a 3D model, learn an uncalibrated flood tool and inspect a riverbank module before explaining the SDP decision. Those are technical enablers. A judge still cannot readily say **who uses this on Monday, what decision changes, and why SDP rather than any city would sponsor it**.

The physical bank module in the hero receives **no hydraulic credit** in the flood solver. The visible reduction comes from hypothetical storage concepts. A combined brief is useful, but it does not make them a single demonstrated flood treatment. The story must stop visually implying a causal connection that the calculations explicitly do not model.

The [organiser’s current brief](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/universityhackathon/) explicitly separates the mechanism—beneficiary and township value—from the technology enabler. Its weights are strategic fit 15%, impact 25%, differentiation 20%, feasibility 20%, commercial value 10%, and team/evidence/pitch 10%. Climate resilience fits Planet, but a generic simulation is insufficient evidence of an SDP implementation mechanism. A live-township pilot is a possibility, not promised adoption.

**Recommended proposition:** “Compare flood-storage ideas before commissioning SDP’s next site investigation.”

**User:** an SDP township asset/development team preparing a drainage or resilience review, supported by a drainage engineer. This is a proposed user to validate, not an existing SDP customer relationship.

**Decision:** does this assumed local-storage idea merit a surveyed feasibility study, and which evidence must the team obtain before it can proceed? Current outputs support an investigation brief. They do not select an optimum construction project, rank investment returns, predict a real storm, identify safe routes or approve works.

**Human consequence:** the community needs functioning access, homes and shared infrastructure. Use documented Bukit Raja flood history to explain why the question matters; do not name specific inundated buildings or invent a resident quote. Keep assumed impacts, historic evidence and modelled grid outputs distinct.

## What actually competes with us

The direct relevance challenge comes from **NEXORA, BlueLoop, HydroResilience, AeroThermal, Prismatic Grid and TownshipOS**: they already put water, assets or operators inside a township narrative. FloodSafe Route, FloodWay and Township Flood System compete for climate-risk attention with a more immediate resident story. Several are source-only, and HydroResilience’s event link is unconfirmed; none is called a validated operating service here.

The strongest decision-story benchmarks are **Surplus AI, Teduh, NgamSoil, HomeLoop and AccessTwin**. Their mechanisms can be stated in one sentence: check before buying; choose where shade budget goes; trace a compost batch; help an owner upgrade; compare an access intervention. Some have access or synthetic-data limits, but their user/decision connection is already clearer than “a 3D resilience workspace”.

LivingShield cannot rely on **3D**, **a digital twin**, **before/after**, **a map of Elmina**, **BM**, or a generic **AI** label as a unique proposition. Those patterns appear in this comparison set. The demonstrated addition worth defending is a transparent, same-forcing local-storage comparison whose assumptions, finite capture and missing evidence survive into a matching township review brief. That is useful if the intended team actually needs it; commercial demand remains unproven.

## Three narrative changes with the highest return

1. **Open with a decision and a specific place.** Lead with a Bukit Raja asset/development review, not the software stack, national coverage or the module film. First screen: proposed user, decision and why this pilot. Put the scenario—not a fabricated real incident—on screen. The judge should be able to say “SDP uses it to decide what to investigate before committing to works”.
2. **Make the first minute a consequential comparison.** Declare 90 mm / 60 minutes as an assumption, run the baseline and finite-storage case, and hold time/camera constant. Show the actual changed wet area and capture, then expose the two partly used basins. Ask “what would you need to know before building this?” Unused capacity creates an engineering question rather than a promotional percentage. A numerical preset does not establish that the proposed sites are buildable.
3. **End with a proposed SDP action.** Export the named-pilot investigation brief with a proposed engineering reviewer, next action and requested survey/drain/boundary evidence. Ask for one township sponsor, a suitable survey window and an engineering mentor to test that workflow. Physical bank inspection can follow as a separate review attachment; it must not imply that the module caused the modelled reduction.

## A tighter 90-second story

- **0–15 seconds:** “Before SDP commits to a flood intervention, which idea deserves the next site investigation? We start with Bandar Bukit Raja because its flood history makes this a real planning question.” Cite the documented history; identify this scenario as assumed.
- **15–50 seconds:** “This is one storm on one terrain window. Here is the baseline. Here are twelve hypothetical local-storage concepts under the same inputs. Compare the same minute.” Run the actual model and read its current result. Explain that the coarse historical surface model still needs survey and calibration.
- **50–70 seconds:** “Some proposed capacity remains empty. A model cannot tell us that those sites are buildable. It tells our reviewer what to question: local inflow, inlet assumptions, drainage connectivity and the boundary.” Show actual explanations rather than an invented confidence score.
- **70–90 seconds:** “The output is a site-investigation brief: proposed reviewer, action, evidence requests, inputs and before/after results. Our pilot asks whether this helps SDP prepare a more reviewable brief with less rework. We need one township sponsor, one survey window and one engineering mentor to test that.” Preparation-time and rework benefits remain hypotheses until measured.

The current 90 mm rehearsal ends with 17,697.755 m³ captured from 18,000 m³ proposed capacity, ten full and two partly used concepts. This is a reproducible **model example**, not a field result or construction design. The bank concept receives no hydraulic/storage credit. An investigation brief is not engineering approval, assignment, a warning service or a purchase request.

## Questions a judge should ask, and the truthful answer

| Judge question | Defensible answer now | Evidence still needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why SDP? | Named SDP pilot windows and an intended asset/development review workflow, keeping the same context through analysis and handover. | Operator interviews, a sponsor and suitable infrastructure/survey information. Names alone establish neither asset ownership nor a buyer. |
| Why use this before a consultant’s hydraulic model? | It prepares assumptions and an investigation brief; it does not replace a calibrated model or regulatory design. | Test the handover against today’s review process and measure preparation/rework. |
| Did the physical module reduce the flood? | No benefit from the bank module is modelled. The displayed reduction comes from hypothetical storage only. | Separate material, connector, anchorage, scour, ecology and hydraulic tests for a bank intervention. |
| What decision can this model approve? | None. It supports a proposed study/review conversation and exports assumptions and gaps. | Survey, real drainage/inflow/boundary conditions, observed-event calibration and qualified review. |
| Does it scale to every township? | The workflow and national context can be reused; detailed geometry/terrain currently covers three pilot windows. | Per-site data suitability, permissions, calibration and adoption. |
| Is the benefit measured? | Numerical accounting and app workflows are tested; operator time, flood loss, biodiversity and commercial gains are not established. | A scoped operator trial with a baseline, sample size and independently reviewed outcomes. |

This review supplies narrative recommendations. It does not claim those recommendations are already implemented in the app or pitch; the parent change and QA must establish that separately. No rival app was modified, private account accessed, or new all-entrant/feature-completeness claim made.
