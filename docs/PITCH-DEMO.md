# LivingShield: the next township investigation

Pitch draft • 21 October 2026, Malaysia time • [Team / university / contact: complete].

**Before SDP commits, show what stays wet.**

The intended users are an SDP township asset manager and the engineer reviewing a case. Their proposed task: compare one storage concept with the same-storm baseline, expose what remains unresolved and scope the next site investigation. This is a product hypothesis; operator adoption and improved decisions have not yet been measured.

SDP already assesses flood risk and invests in adaptation. Its [2025 Sustainability Report, pp87–88](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/SDP%20SR%202025%20Mobile.pdf#page=93) names BBR and BUP in its assessment and describes adaptation works and drainage maintenance. Our proposed place is the handover from a risk finding to a specific investigation brief. We have not imported SDP's internal assessment or established an endorsed process.

The [official challenge](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/universityhackathon/) asks for a mechanism and a technology enabler. Lead with the asset-manager-to-engineer decision. The map, simulation and physical concept help examine it. Proposal submission is **23 October 2026**, Top 10 pitch day **14 November**, and final presentation/ceremony **3 December**; confirm exact participant instructions. No registration or submission is performed by this document.

## Three-minute demonstration

**0:00–0:25 — Give the judge an SDP decision.**

“Put yourself in SDP's township team. A storage idea is on the table. Before commissioning the next site investigation, what evidence would you ask for? LivingShield lets the asset manager and engineer review that case together.”

Choose **Start the guided demo**. Use Bandar Bukit Raja throughout. The 225 ha pilot is a locality window, not the entire township or a surveyed catchment. Historical flood context does not identify inundated buildings in this scene. Avoid opening with software features, nationwide dataset counts or module dimensions.

**0:25–1:20 — Make one choice visible.**

Open **2 · Compare the storm**. Set manual rainfall **90 mm**, duration **60 minutes**, coefficient **0.70**, assumed drainage **5 mm/h**, Manning n **0.04** and **Free outfall; no external inflow**. Run **Run flood comparison**, choose **Place pooling-point demo basins**, then rerun. Pause at **60 minutes**. Collapse the guide and enter **Present view**. Toggle **Before mitigation** / **After mitigation** at the same camera and timestamp.

“This is the same place and assumed storm. We changed local storage. At this frame, modelled wet area changes from **38.5 to 33.2 ha**. Water still remains after the proposal. That is the question to take into the site investigation.”

The areas use the ≥0.10 m grid-depth threshold. They are uncalibrated scenario results, not parcel inundation or validated protection.

**1:20–1:50 — Turn the result into questions.**

Open **Why this result?** Show actual capture and the water budget.

“By the end, twelve demo basins capture about **17,698 of 18,000 m³**. Ten reach capacity; two are partly used. We need to check site levels, actual drain and inlet connections, and appropriate boundary water levels before relying on this layout.”

End capture is different from capture at the selected playback time. Partial filling alone cannot identify the binding constraint. Conservation checks the accounting; observed-event calibration is still needed.

**1:50–2:35 — Show the township team's next action.**

Use **Review this flood option** to go directly to Evidence. A flood-only brief is useful; missing bank evidence remains explicit. Choose **Prepare the local review**, select **Flood-model review**, propose **Hydraulic engineer**, and enter:

> Verify surveyed levels, drainage connectivity and boundary conditions for this pilot before reviewing the storage layout.

Select the evidence requests needed for that conversation. Prepared is a browser-local draft status, not an assignment or approval. Download the readable brief and show that the inputs, actual results and next action travel together. Full JSON retains the frames and sources.

**Optional 15-second follow-up, outside the timed route:** if asked about River Lab, show the module and change one bank dimension. “This is one separate design candidate with quantities and test needs. It receives no simulated storage or flood-depth reduction credit.” Do not make the bank concept the explanation for the before/after change.

**2:35–3:00 — Ask for a testable next step.**

“Our first ask is one suitable SDP case, an asset-team sponsor, an engineering reviewer and an authorised data pack. In a bounded pilot we will compare brief-preparation time, unaided completion and review completeness against the current process. Each next township needs its own data and review.”

Close with [team/contact]. Do not claim measured homes protected, avoided losses, return on investment, bank stability or habitat gains.

If a live calculation takes too long, disclose that the visible comparison was computed earlier in the same session and show its inputs and timestamp. Never substitute a fixed benefit percentage. Event peaks may occur at different times; use the matched-frame values for the live comparison. [Numerical verification](FLOOD-MODEL-VALIDATION.md) records the engineering scope.

## Sixty-second pitch

“An SDP township asset manager has a storage idea to review. What evidence should the engineer ask for before the next investigation?

LivingShield makes that case inspectable. In this 225-hectare Bukit Raja window, hold the assumed storm fixed and add twelve hypothetical basins. The model captures about 17,698 cubic metres by the end, but at 60 minutes about 33.2 hectares still meet its wet-area threshold.

That remaining water leads to the next questions: actual drains, inlet connections and boundary levels. Export the assumptions, result and proposed review action together.

The bank module is a separate candidate requiring tests, with no assumed flood benefit.

Our ask: one SDP case and two reviewing roles to test whether this produces a clearer investigation brief faster.”

Read figures only when the matching case is visible. These are uncalibrated scenario results, not actual homes inundated or protected.

## Questions a judge is likely to ask

| Question | Defensible answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why SDP? | The intended user manages a township asset case. The deliverable is a scoped investigation with explicit assumptions, candidate results and requested evidence, prepared for an engineer. The pilot tests its value in that task. |
| Does SDP not already have maps and consultants? | Yes. We propose to help the asset team prepare the case it sends to qualified engineers and must compare it with the existing process. This prototype does not replace detailed hydraulic work or model the complete installed drainage network. |
| What differs from other projects? | Our demonstrated combination joins a same-storm comparison, finite capture, remaining wet area and exported review action in one context. Other projects also offer 3D, scenarios and handovers; uniqueness is not established. |
| Are those homes really flooded? | The window is real, but the overlay is an assumed storm on coarse historical surface elevations. It cannot establish a home's inundation or safety. |
| Why 3D? | It provides a shared spatial reference for the asset manager and reviewer. We have not yet measured whether it improves task time or decisions. The numerical evidence remains exportable. |
| Why include the bank module? | It is one separate design candidate with quantities and test needs. It receives no storage or flood-depth reduction credit. A storage-only first pilot can scope bank testing separately. |
| Can SDP build these basins? | The preset is a demonstration. Land control, site levels, connectivity, boundaries, installed assets and engineering feasibility still require checking. |
| Where are the savings? | Not measured. The trial measures briefing time, completeness and usability. Avoided flood losses, construction savings and willingness to pay are not established. |
| How does it scale? | Repeat an accepted case template, with suitable local terrain, assets, boundary data and engineering review for each township. National data gives context with uneven coverage. |
| What do you need now? | One suitable case, an asset-team sponsor, an engineering reviewer, authorised data and agreement on the baseline task and acceptance criteria. |

## Model and source boundaries to retain

- The flood model uses coarse, historical Copernicus **surface** elevations, approximately 46.9 m cells. Drains/sewers, culverts, individual building barriers, river/tidal and neighbouring-catchment inflows are unresolved. Depths are uncalibrated grid outputs, not validated parcel/floor levels, safe routes or warnings. Most displayed building heights and facades remain illustrative. The separate volume screen uses ideal-capture arithmetic.
- The physical model is a design concept, not a certified revetment or construction specification. Nominal 300 mm dimensions differ from measured GLB bounds of 309.865 × 308.991 × 278.267 mm. Straight-bank geometry, planting and installation stages are illustrative. The original animation’s stabilization, slower-flow and habitat statements are design intentions; its animated water is not a hydraulic experiment. No suitable Malaysian species, surveyed bank alignment or structural performance is established.
- Bank quantities use nominal triangular packing, complete rows and an editable spare allowance. Module-only subtotals exclude installation, planting, anchors, site works, delivery and tax. Unknown quotations remain unknown. A bank concept and a storage-basin comparison appearing in the same brief do not establish a combined flood benefit.
- National observations, model context and regional agency inventories have different coverage, age and terms. Missing data does not establish no infrastructure or no risk. River gauge height is not site flood depth. Public JPS feeds are not documented stable API contracts; confirm provider terms and current JPS/PBT requirements before operational use.
