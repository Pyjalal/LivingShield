# Public demo visual benchmark

Checked 21 October 2026. This review covers public self-identified projects and a separate group of unconfirmed candidates, not an organiser roster. The original **Browser Agent/Jev desktop pass attempted 27 published non-video pages across 23 projects** and inspected their actual screenshots. Later first-hand Playwright checks add specific functional and mobile evidence in the [primary supplement](COMPETITOR-FUNCTIONAL-CHECKS.md) and [secondary supplement](COMPETITOR-FUNCTIONAL-SECONDARY.md). Those reports supersede earlier limits only for the actions and states they actually tested.

In the initial 27-page Jev pass, fourteen runs completed, eight exhausted the decision budget, three were blocked, one failed due to an invalid model response, and one was cancelled after a visible deployment 404. Four later Jev app-entry attempts add three budget-exhausted runs and one completed guest-entry URL check. The Jev video run inspected Givlah's opening at 0:01 of 3:08; a separate [sampled recording review](GIVLAH-VIDEO-OBSERVATIONS.md) later inspected nine views spanning about 0:21–3:01. Jev status counts describe their recorded attempts, not the complete implementation or later functional/video results.

## Method and limits

The original Jev pass used short tasks at 1440px: inspect the initial page and one usable navigation flow without accounts, form submissions or data changes. The initial demo pass permits six or seven Jev decisions; the first two follow-up retries permit six; later QA entry checks have their own recorded bounds. Source-page checks use four or fewer decisions and read-only goals. A URL assertion verifies only location, not advertised functionality. These Jev app-page records are desktop-only. The later supplements record their own tested flows, viewport sizes and outcomes; the primary supplement includes eight 390px mobile captures. Neither phase establishes complete mobile accessibility, backend correctness, every calculation or full end-to-end operations.

The reviewer inspects saved screenshots and action reports. A score is an observed-scene design assessment, not hackathon judging or a prediction of winning. Scale: 1 = major readability/structure problems; 3 = clear, conventional usable presentation; 5 = polished hierarchy and an unusually clear, well-integrated experience. Hierarchy, visual coherence and task clarity are scored independently where a product screen was visible. Unavailable/error pages and documentation-only projects do not receive app-function scores.

The [secondary first-hand supplement](COMPETITOR-FUNCTIONAL-SECONDARY.md) records the remaining 15 public projects and 30 inspected desktop/mobile captures. AccessTwin and GreenLoop should be judged using their later reached workspaces as well as the earlier Jev history. Together, the two supplements cover all 23 discovered public projects with a named observed interaction or actual access boundary. The [Givlah recording supplement](GIVLAH-VIDEO-OBSERVATIONS.md) separately inspects nine timestamped views. This adds recording evidence without adding Jev runs or a mobile-browser pass.

## Product decisions from observed evidence

Use one master brand, common typography and consistent navigation throughout the project. A calm landing should present one problem, one township and one obvious entry action. Inside the 3D workspace, move secondary nationwide data, assumptions and provenance behind progressive disclosure. Keep the same camera, storm and time when comparing mitigation; make differences explicit with scene-linked values. Join the house report, operator response and township evidence into one guided story. These are opportunities to improve our own demo; no public evidence establishes a guaranteed win or complete competitor coverage.
