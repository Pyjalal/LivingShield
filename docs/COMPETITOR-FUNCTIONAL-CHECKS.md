# Public demo functional checks

Checked 21 October 2026 in an isolated Chromium session with Playwright CLI. This is a supplemental first-hand review of eight public demos from the visual benchmark, not a judging prediction or a complete test of their implementations. Primary sources are the linked, rendered public applications. The observations below supersede the earlier short-run limitations only for the flows explicitly checked here.

Desktop checks used 1440 × 1000. Mobile captures used 390 × 844 and cover the named first screen or workspace only. Every screenshot listed in the accompanying [JSON ledger](competitor-functional-checks.json) was opened and inspected. Local artifacts are under `app/output/competitor-functional/`; this ignored QA directory is not a deployable dependency. The ledger records the absolute artifact root and filenames.

Only public navigation, filtering, local scenario inputs and explicitly advertised demo entry were used. No accounts were created, credentials guessed, private profiles accessed, messages sent, reports submitted, holds approved, appointments booked, items borrowed or payments initiated. NEXORA and PinjamLah were entered through their explicit public demo-account buttons. Read-only here permits a demo sign-in session and browser-local settings, but not changing remote operational records.

## Comparison with the current LivingShield implementation

The source comparison reviewed `app/src/township/TownshipView.tsx`, `app/src/platform/GuidedDemo.tsx`, `app/src/platform/EvidenceView.tsx`, `app/src/platform/readableBrief.ts` and the native River Lab. It does not substitute for the separate local-app QA. LivingShield already has a four-step, state-aware guide, terrain-based before/after comparisons with matching forcing, explicit model limits, actual module inspection, editable quantities, and a combined JSON/readable evidence handover. These should be demonstrated clearly before adding more modules. None of the eight limited competitor flows establishes that our physical-design export or hydraulic screening is unique or validated.

At the time of this baseline review, three meaningful follow-up gaps remained candidates:

1. **Explain the decision beside the computed result.** Surplus AI's working/evidence panel makes the current recommendation inspectable without reading a separate methodology document. LivingShield exposes sources and water balance, but could join each selected intervention to its actual local capture limit, exhausted capacity, remaining wet area and uncertainty in a compact explanation. Reuse the solver's outputs; do not invent an AI confidence percentage.
2. **Make the pilot handover actionable.** TamanKu's sample tracking timeline and NEXORA's linked alert/job context show who should do the next step. LivingShield's brief could carry an explicitly local review status, proposed reviewer and required site evidence, while retaining the existing warning that there is no agency submission or construction approval. A fabricated live operations queue would weaken the evidence.
3. **Provide a focused Bahasa Malaysia explanation path.** Kita4U's working BM switch demonstrates a concrete access improvement. A reviewed bilingual guided narrative and brief summary would help Malaysian stakeholders without expanding the engineering model or claiming translations are already available.

No probability of winning, complete competitor feature ranking, production readiness, full mobile accessibility pass or real-world performance validation is inferred from this review.

Subsequent implementation: result-specific capture/accounting, a browser-local review plan and a focused bilingual guided narrative now address these gaps. See [the upgrade verification](COMPETITIVE-UPGRADE-VERIFICATION.md) for the exact implemented scope, numerical checks, production flow and remaining limits. The readable brief remains English.
